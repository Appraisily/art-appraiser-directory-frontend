## Design Context

### Users

The primary users are collectors, heirs, estate executors, donors, insurance
customers, and owners who need to find a credible fine-art appraiser near a
specific city. They are usually non-experts making a consequential decision:
who to contact, whether local or online appraisal fits, and what evidence or
report they need. The interface must remain useful on a phone and under time or
decision pressure.

The directory has two jobs:

1. Help visitors compare the small set of providers whose identity, location,
   and fine-art relevance have been reviewed.
2. Give visitors one clear next step when local coverage is unavailable:
   continue to Appraisily's existing appraisal or screener flow without
   changing its pricing or checkout behavior.

### Brand Personality

Quietly authoritative, editorial, and evidence-led. The directory should feel
like a well-run appraisal office or art reference desk: calm enough for a
collector, precise enough for an executor, and plain enough for a first-time
visitor. Copy is Appraisily brand copy with Andres-style clarity, not a personal
message from Andres.

Avoid unsupported certification, review, authenticity, insurer-acceptance, IRS,
or outcome claims. Name the customer's decision risk, show the evidence that is
actually available, and offer one next action.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use a light, refined editorial direction grounded in art documentation rather
than software decoration:

- warm paper-white page field;
- deep ink/navy authority color;
- restrained oxblood or museum-red detail for the directory's distinct
  editorial accent;
- sage only for genuinely reviewed/verified status;
- serif-led display hierarchy paired with a highly readable sans-serif body;
- fine rules, generous white space, archival-paper and frame cues;
- one dominant art/report image when imagery adds evidence;
- initials/monogram tiles when provider photography is missing.

Keep the existing Appraisily identity intact at downstream Appraisily
destinations, while giving this directory one compact, non-duplicated wordmark
and matching favicon. Do not imitate generic blue SaaS dashboards, stock-photo
marketplaces, luxury gradients, glassmorphism, equal icon-card grids, or noisy
badge systems.

Theme: light only for this focused refresh. Motion must be subtle, fast,
purposeful, and disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

### Design Principles

1. **Inventory truth before presentation.** Only reviewed providers and cities
   appear in discovery surfaces; design never disguises missing or suppressed
   data.
2. **Evidence earns emphasis.** Provider facts, source dates, methodology, and
   report examples outrank adjectives, ratings, or decorative trust badges.
3. **One decision per block.** Every section answers a question, proves
   credibility, or moves the visitor to one next action.
4. **Editorial, not ornamental.** Typography, spacing, rules, art, and document
   structure create character; gradients, generic icons, and repeated cards do
   not.
5. **Complete states are part of the design.** Missing images, unavailable
   cities, denied geolocation, empty contacts, errors, loading, focus, and
   reduced motion receive intentional accessible treatments.
6. **Mobile is an adapted composition.** Navigation, search, provider evidence,
   and the primary CTA remain usable without cramped wordmarks or hidden
   functionality.
7. **WCAG AA is the minimum.** Maintain high contrast, visible focus, semantic
   structure, keyboard operation, 44px touch targets, and non-color status
   labels.
